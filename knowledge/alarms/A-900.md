# A-900 · Part jam detected

**Any station** — the event carries the station that raised it · **Severity** fault · a part
is stuck. The station goes `Held` and requires an operator to Unhold.

## What it means

`Held` makes this a **cause candidate** wherever it is raised. Jams are also the main source
of this line's routine short interruptions, so most instances of A-900 are cleared quickly and
never become line stops.

## Micro-stop or stop

This is the distinction that matters for A-900, and it is decided by **output**, not by the
alarm: if no part left S4 for longer than 60 s, it is a line stop; otherwise it is a
micro-stop. A jam cleared in twenty seconds is a micro-stop even though a station was `Held`
and an operator was involved.

Answering a micro-stop question with a stop investigation is a wrong answer. Say what it was
first, then say what happened.

## Check

1. Which station raised it, and how long it stood before acknowledgement.
2. Whether jams are clustering — on one station, one period, or one carrier. A rising
   micro-stop count is a diagnostic signal in its own right (DP-09) even when no single
   instance is significant.
3. Whether the jammed part is accounted for. A part removed by hand may have no completion
   record, and it must not be silently absent from a containment scope.

## What it does not mean

One jam is not a trend. This line runs brief jams roughly every twenty minutes as its normal
condition, so a single A-900 is background, not a finding. Compare against that baseline before
reporting anything.

See also: DP-09, SOP-01, A-202.
