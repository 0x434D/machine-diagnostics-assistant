# A-207 · Joining force out of tolerance

**Station** S2 Joining · **Severity** fault · peak joining force left its tolerance band.
S2 goes `Aborted` and requires Clearing then Reset, so an operator was involved.

## What it means

A **cause candidate**, and the one that most often is the cause. When a stop chain terminates
at S2 `Aborted` with A-207 standing, the category is `internal` and the root is S2.

## Check

1. **The force trend across the preceding hours, not just the alarm instant.** A drift that
   walked out of tolerance and a step that jumped out of it are different faults. The drift
   shape is scenario 3 and it is usually visible long before the alarm, with `gap` defects
   rising ahead of it (DP-01).
2. Which direction. Force falling with `gap` rising is the drift-down shape; force rising with
   `crack` appearing is overforce.
3. **Whether parts were affected before the alarm.** The alarm fires at the tolerance limit,
   but parts pressed below it and above nominal were already being made. Scope those parts
   (SOP-04) — they are the ones that shipped.
4. Whether the drift correlates with a lot. If the *force* is drifting, it should not.

## What it does not mean

**A-207 is not proof that the press is the cause of a quality problem.** The reverse trap is
the one this line is built to test: rising `gap` with a *stable* force is a material problem
(DP-05), and A-207 will not be standing in that case. The absence of this alarm during a
`gap` rise is itself evidence.

## The question that must be asked with this alarm

"How many parts passed S2 while the force was out of tolerance, and how many of them shipped?"
That is the answer someone needs at three in the morning, and it comes from the affected-parts
query, not from the trend.

See also: S2, A-201, `gap`, `crack`, DP-01, DP-05, SOP-04.
