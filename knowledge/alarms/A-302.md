# A-302 · Illumination below reference

**Station** S3 Inspection · **Severity** warning · the illumination reference has fallen
below its threshold. Inspection continues.

## What it means

The imaging chain is degrading while still producing verdicts, which makes this the most
diagnostically dangerous alarm in the set — **the results keep coming and they are quietly
worth less.**

This is the alarm that accompanies optics fouling. The signature to expect downstream is
confidence decaying across all six classes with the scrap rate roughly flat (DP-04), because
the classifier becomes less certain about everything rather than finding more of anything.

## Check

1. **Run the known-good master part.** This is the documented first move and it settles the
   question: a master part that no longer scores clean means the imaging changed, not the
   production.
2. The illumination reference as a trend. A gradual decline is fouling or ageing; a step is
   something that happened — a lamp, a guard, a setting.
3. Verdict confidence across the same window, for the whole population rather than for
   rejects.

## What it does not mean

It does not mean the parts got worse, and it does not mean they got better. **Every defect
rate computed over a window where this alarm stood is suspect**, in both directions: false
rejects rise, and genuine defects can be missed. Say that plainly in any answer covering such
a window rather than reporting the rates as if they were sound.

## What to do with rates in this window

State the caveat, give the figures, and do not silently correct them. There is no defensible
way to adjust a rate for unknown imaging degradation, and an adjusted number with an
invisible assumption is worse than an honest caveated one.

See also: S3, DP-04, `contamination`, `scratch`, CORE-02.
